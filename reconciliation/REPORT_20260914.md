# Green Lory–Green Porpoise reconciliation report

14 September 2026. ARC status checked at **11:39 BST**; solver-bound snapshot
from 11:29 BST. Mt/year means million
tonnes of ammonia per year. Costs are USD2018-equivalent unless stated otherwise;
they are not current-dollar estimates.

## Executive conclusion

The archived Green Porpoise project can now be reproduced, and both new global
Green Lory surfaces have completed and passed independent checks. The principal
discrepancy is **not simply a higher LCOA**: the new land-derived capacities and
the 1 Mt/year supplier cutoff remove much of the supplier base supporting the
historical network. Network-equation and demand-input differences are a second,
independent source of disagreement.

| Outcome | Current status |
|---|---|
| Reproduce the archived project with its recorded surface and equations | Achieved: 601.580 Mt/year and USD 254.05/t; original and sparse implementations agree |
| Reproduce the actual published MOD-AMB network | Incomplete: deposited code is recovered, but the publication's exact numerical input snapshot is not pinned |
| Produce a better-founded new plant surface and network | Full candidate plant surface completed and checked; network comparisons running; spatial assumptions still require validation |

My recommendation is to retain a frozen historical reproduction and a separately
versioned improved model. The hourly, explicit-compressor, tank-only configuration
is the better starting point for the latter. It is **not yet justified to call
its geographic supplier pattern more realistic**, particularly in Australia.

## 1. Scope and the meaning of reproduction

The primary endpoint is **2050 MOD-AMB: RCP4.5/SSP2, 70% ammonia adoption, Way
2050 technology costs, no subsidies and no vessel-efficiency improvement**.
RCP4.5/90% remains a separate project benchmark. It must not be presented as
the paper's HIGH-AMB scenario, which is RCP2.6/90%.

Green Porpoise consumes costs, capacities, supplier identities and countries,
demand, and transport data. A cost surface can match closely while its network
changes radically. Reproduction therefore requires a versioned input bundle and
formulation, not a map that looks similar.

There are two different meanings of historical reproduction:

1. **Replay the recorded inputs.** This has been achieved for the archived
   project and is the dependable regression reference.
2. **Regenerate those inputs from weather, technology and land assumptions.**
   The new historical-assumption plant calculation approximates the old costs,
   but does not reproduce the old capacities. This task is not complete.

- Made historical time-step accounting and physical-duration accounting explicit,
  including ramp-rate interpretation and consistent result reporting.
- Separated compressor capital cost from hydrogen storage cost. The candidate
  central case pairs explicit compression with a DEA 2050 tank-only store.
- Implemented and tested renewable land accounting, including tracking PV
  footprint and technology-specific land limits. Historical and revised modes
  remain identifiable.
- Ran four geographic shards for each of two global surfaces, plus two controlled
  three-cell diagnostic cases, followed by merge/QA jobs.
- Retrieved completed ARC results on 14 September and independently rechecked
  inputs, outputs, hashes, coordinates and physical accounting.

### Green Porpoise implementation and execution

- Replayed the original archived equations on ARC. The first solve was optimal
  but failed during export because Pyomo's JSON writer pruned zero-valued solution
  entries before loading. Loading the solution first fixed the export; a
  regression test covers an inactive supplier. The failed attempt is retained.
- Built a sparse equivalent removing banned pipeline variables. Its archived
  objective agrees with the original implementation within USD 0.001/year.
  Variable count falls from 7,414,740 to 2,022,330; constraints from roughly
  5.53 million to 25,624.
- Checked original/sparse parity on small networks for both formulations,
  including cases that must use maritime transport.
- Added strict supplier export contracts: global coverage, currency/year,

- Submitted five deposited-formulation network comparisons, detailed below.

The current Green Lory test suite passed **80 tests**. This verifies tested

## 3. Green Lory findings

### 3.1 Both full surfaces pass internal checks

Each surface contains **52,702 expected coordinates**, representing the full
8,760-hour year at its specified temporal resolution. Revalidation found no
duplicate, missing or unexpected coordinates and no shard-failure sidecars.
Downloaded results match recorded hashes and their remerged shard contents.

Independent calculations found:

- Zero grid-import energy in both surfaces.
- Feasibility-penalty energy below 0.001 MWh/year.
- Reference production within 0.00005 t/year of the intended 1 Mt/year.
- Renewable-land capacity reconstruction error below 0.000001 t/year.
- Plant component-cost closure below 0.000000001 EUR/t, with central water
  cost accounted for separately and consistently.

These checks guard against missing runs, unit mistakes and inconsistent
post-processing. They do not establish that the land budget is empirically correct.

### 3.2 Replication costs are close in the middle; capacities are not

Across **14,035 matched cells with positive historical and replication
capacity**, replication LCOA differs from the historical table by **−0.97% at
the median**. The 10th–90th percentile range is −11.60% to +2.78%, so this is
not pointwise equality. The median replication/historical capacity ratio is
only **0.217**.

| Cell | Historical LCOA, USD/t | Replication LCOA | Central LCOA | Historical capacity, Mt/year | Replication capacity | Central capacity |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Atacama, −23°, −69° | 212.92 | 215.54 | 246.93 | 9.769 | 2.316 | 2.312 |
| Northwest Australia, −23°, 117° | 233.69 | 235.51 | 266.85 | 4.020 | 0.955 | 0.951 |
| Central Australia, −21°, 135° | 226.39 | 220.23 | 231.20 | 4.324 | 0.459 | 0.397 |

Both Australian cells cease to be candidates under the 1 Mt/year cutoff,
although their replication costs remain close to historical values.

Lory's EUR2020 figures are divided by **0.9024**, the inverse of the documented
Way USD2018-to-EUR2020 conversion. Current exchange rates were not substituted.

### 3.3 Supplier eligibility changes the feasible geography

| Measure | Historical surface | Replication surface | Hourly central surface |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Unique cells at or above 1 Mt/year, before cheapest-4,000 selection | 4,548 | 1,253 | 832 |
| Eligible Australian cells | 553 | 22 | 1 |
| Eligible Australian capacity, Mt/year | 1,762.15 | 25.01 | 1.02 |
| Historically active suppliers now below cutoff | — | 166 of 234 | 188 of 234 |
| Historical production at those excluded cells, Mt/year | — | 408.48 | 474.10 |

Eligible capacity is a potential upper bound, not actual production or exports.
The historical capacity total is before the optimizer's additional 10 Mt/year
per-site cap. The old row-selection procedure selected 4,000 rows but only
3,996 unique IDs because of four duplicated border IDs. The table above counts
unique coordinates before that selection.

Nineteen historically eligible coordinates are absent from the new surfaces
because their input land capacity is zero, not because ARC tasks are missing.
All 234 active historical suppliers do have new results. No supplier pool was
padded to 4,000 and no capacity was promoted artificially to the cutoff.

The archived network produces **258.09 Mt/year in Australia**. An input bundle
allowing only 25.01 Mt/year there—or 1.02 Mt/year in central—cannot reproduce
that output, whatever network optimizer is used. This is a feasibility limit,
not a conjecture about optimization behaviour.

### 3.4 Why central plant costs rise

For the same 1,253 replication-eligible cells, central LCOA is **12.61% higher
at the median**; the 10th–90th percentile increase is 8.92–14.14%.

Controlled increments, in USD2018-equivalent per tonne:

| Cell | Hourly resolution and ramp/time accounting | Explicit compressor | Replace bundled H2 store with tank-only | Water | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Atacama | +10.69 | +21.45 | −3.65 | +2.91 | +31.40 |
| Northwest Australia | +9.28 | +22.91 | −3.77 | +2.91 | +31.34 |
| Central Australia | +2.62 | +6.72 | −1.29 | +2.91 | +10.97 |

These are **sequential, order-dependent effects at three locations**, not a
global decomposition. The first step bundles temporal resolution and time/ramp
accounting; it does not isolate each separately.

The explicit-compressor-plus-legacy-store intermediate carries a double-counting
risk and is an attribution case only. Central uses DEA 2050 tank-only capital cost
of EUR 15,300/MWh, replacing the legacy EUR 18,700/MWh store, alongside explicit
compressor capital cost. Catalogue boundaries still need a final engineering
cross-check. Uniform water adds USD 3/t on its USD2020 input basis, approximately
USD2018 2.91/t after conversion.

### 3.5 Land is the critical realism uncertainty

Land assumptions include a uniform 2% competition allowance, protected-area and
15° slope exclusions, land-class suitability, latitude-adjusted PV packing, a
tracking footprint twice the fixed-PV footprint, and 5 MW/km² wind density.
Shared renewable land is a classwise nested-overlap lower-bound estimate, not
an exact pixelwise union. Replication uses the union budget; central additionally
imposes technology-specific onshore limits.

At fixed optimized plant designs and unchanged land availability:

| Design | Global eligible cells if fixed-PV packing is applied to tracking | With specified tracking packing | Australian fixed-packing count | Australian tracking count |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Replication | 2,167 | 1,253 | 175 | 22 |
| Central | 1,688 | 832 | 66 | 1 |

This does not justify using fixed-PV spacing for tracking. It shows that
Australia's apparent disappearance depends strongly on footprint assumptions
interacting with a sharp cutoff. Adding technology-specific onshore limits to
the tracking union-only calculation changes **neither surface's eligible count**;
it is not the cause of the 1,253-to-832 change.

Tracking systems can need different spacing, but a universal twofold
capacity-footprint multiplier is not self-validating. NREL's empirical work
distinguishes direct from whole-site land use and capacity from energy
normalization; these definitions must match the model before calibration.
[NREL land-use study](https://research-hub.nlr.gov/en/publications/land-use-requirements-for-solar-power-plants-in-the-united-states/).

The method optimizes a reference plant and then scales it to land; it does not
re-optimize the renewable mix for every finite site. It also uses one weather
year. Both limitations matter before making strong spatial claims.

### 4.2 Deposited code is not the later project model

| Issue | Public deposited implementation | Later archived project | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Demand energy conversion | Preprocessor uses 39/18.8 | Uses 39/18.6 | Different ammonia demand from the same fuel file |
| Port activation | Maritime flow activates a binary | Reversed inequality can leave it zero | Archived model can bypass minimum maritime storage |
| Maritime storage | At least 1.5 Panamax loads at an active port | Minimum can be bypassed | Cost and transshipment incentives differ |
| Import/export exclusivity | Not imposed | Present but ineffective with zero binaries | Formulations are not equivalent |
| Additional global demand | No extra global lower bound | Includes unmapped port demand | Extra production has no destination-specific requirement |
| Pipeline unit cost | USD 0.025612/t/km | USD 0.0256/t/km | Small but explicit difference |
| Solver gap | Public default 1.5% | Current comparisons target 0.1% | Cost and map comparisons must account for tolerance |

Additional discrepancies remain explicit:

- The public demand preprocessor uses ammonia energy content 18.8, but the
  public optimizer's physical-property dictionary lists 18.6.
- Paper prose describes 10% pipeline overdesign; recovered archived distances
  are approximately great-circle distance without that factor.
- Prose describes a small-port storage cap of one year's demand; deposited code
  retains an uncapped 1.5-Panamax minimum.
- Deposited storage cost is calculated per cubic metre but applied to mass-valued
  storage variables without density conversion.
- Country metadata at duplicated border cells can affect route eligibility.
- Original and later generated pipeline tensors are not interchangeable. The
  later tensor identified in the project removes all route bans; identity must
  be pinned, not inferred from a similar filename.

Source reproduction must preserve its equations. A paper-prose variant and a
physically/unit-corrected variant should be separate comparisons.

## 5. Which results are more realistic?

**At plant level:** central is more defensible in its hourly variability,
duration-aware accounting and explicit compressor/tank cost boundaries. Higher
costs have identifiable mechanisms and controlled diagnostic support. It is the
preferred candidate for further development.

**At spatial supply level:** no definitive realism ranking is established. The
new capacity calculation is internally consistent, but PV footprint, uniform
land share, the overlap approximation and discrete cutoff can suppress whole
regions. Historical large capacities are not validated merely by reproducing
the old map either.

**At network level:** deposited port activation is more consistent with its
minimum-storage requirement than the reversed archived inequality. Deposited
code still has unit/prose ambiguities and incomplete input provenance. A
corrected network is a new formulation, not a silent historical replacement.

Both plant surfaces retain optimistic Way learning, reduced WACC, flat build
and remoteness assumptions, no land rent and uniform water cost. Central is
a better-specified **conditional 2050 estimate**, not a comprehensive real-world
cost forecast. Higher cost alone is not evidence of greater realism.

## 6. Next steps, in order

### Green Lory

1. **Freeze both historical references.** Keep the archived surface as the exact
   replay input and the regenerated four-hour surface as a separately named
   historical-assumption emulation. Do not label the latter an exact historical
   supply-capacity reconstruction.
2. **Close the historical-generation gap.** Recover original weather, technology,
   land and solver/version configuration where possible. Explain cost residuals
   and capacity differences with controlled substitutions. Do not adjust land
   availability to restore a desired Australian result.
3. **Validate land definitions before another global plant sequence.** Check
   PV AC/DC basis, whole-site versus direct area, tracker type, spacing/shading
   and latitude; wind spacing and possible co-use; protected/slope intersections;
   and the empirical basis of the 2% allowance.
4. **Test the supplier cutoff.** Compare 0.25, 0.5 and 1 Mt/year thresholds and
   regional aggregation of neighboring resources. Historical reproduction keeps
   1 Mt/year; lower thresholds are explicit model changes.
5. **Re-optimize selected finite sites.** Test proportional scaling of the
   unconstrained reference design, particularly around Australia's cutoff. A
   finite site may select a different renewable mix. Check scale dependence
   before treating a smaller plant as a linear copy.
6. **Validate operating and cost assumptions.** Test multiple weather years and
   adverse periods; audit Haber–Bosch turndown/ramping, storage pressure and
   compressor boundaries, lifetimes, WACC and water supply. Keep engineering
   corrections separate from learning and financing scenarios.

### Green Porpoise

1. **Finish and validate the five runs.** Fetch summaries and flows; check demand
   coverage, supplier bounds, mass balance, port activation/storage, cost closure
   and solver gaps. Retain time-limited feasible solutions as provisional rather
   than weakening the acceptance target after seeing results.
2. **Resolve publication provenance.** Locate the exact five input datasets,
   configuration and preferably output flows. If unavailable, describe the
   outcome as a bounded reconstruction. An author/data request can be prepared;
   none has been sent without authorization.
3. **Complete the attribution matrix.** With identical demand, routing and
   candidate identities, cross old/new costs with old/new capacities. Report
   common-support comparisons separately from full-pool eligibility effects.
   Do not invent costs for the 19 zero-land cells or hide historical fallbacks.
   Full attribution may require additional historical-style diagnostic runs
   for those coordinates.
4. **Separate equation changes.** Compare deposited-code, paper-prose and
   unit-corrected variants. Isolate storage/activation, density conversion,
   small-port cap, demand conversion and unmatched ports, route eligibility
   and the 1.1 multiplier. Hold maritime costs fixed until explicitly tested.
5. **Compare networks quantitatively.** Measure production by country and
   Australian subregion, imports/exports, active suppliers and ports, flows,
   costs and demand served. Examine alternative near-optimal networks: a
   different individual supplier need not imply a materially different system.
6. **Promote only a documented result.** Keep archival replay, publication
   reconstruction and revised model separate, each with hashes, configurations,
   run IDs, bounds and comparisons. Do not replace defaults with central before
   these checks.

The final explanation should follow this chain:

**plant costs and operating constraints → renewable/storage design → land-limited
capacity → supplier eligibility → routing/storage → network cost and geography.**

That chain is already well supported through supplier eligibility. Controlled
final network attribution is the next unfinished part.
