# Land availability: reconstruction, diagnosis and next model

14 September 2026. This is an addendum to the September reconciliation report,
not a replacement for its frozen results.

## Conclusions so far

The current central land input is **not a validated improvement merely because
its capacities are smaller**. I found real spatial and packing implementation
problems. However, correcting those problems does **not** explain away the
widespread difference from the archived ammonia capacities. At the three main
diagnostic locations, corrected suitable land is fairly close to the current
land budget; the much larger discrepancies occur when land is converted into
ammonia capacity.

The paper's method is a reasonable, reproducible screening framework. It is not
a direct observation of developable land, and its class fractions and uniform
2% shipping allocation remain assumptions. A correctly implemented version is
the right replication comparator; it should not be forced to reproduce the
archived capacity table by adjusting land fractions.

Fixed PV is a sensible candidate for the revised model, but it must have its
own generation profile and optimized plant design. Applying fixed-PV spacing
to a tracking plant while retaining tracking output is only a diagnostic.

## What the references actually establish

The paper's section 4.7 and Supplementary Table 2 specify land-class suitability,
protected-area exclusions, a slope limit of 15 degrees, renewable footprints,
and a subsequent 2% allocation to shipping. Table 2 matches the current
onshore class factors:

| Land category | Solar fraction | Onshore wind fraction |
|---|---:|---:|
| Forests, wetlands, snow/ice, water | 0 | 0 |
| Closed/open shrublands | 0.50 | 0.50 |
| Woody savanna, savanna, grassland | 0.20 | 0.20 |
| Cropland and crop/natural mosaics | 0 | 0.05 |
| Urban/built-up | 0.03 | 0 |
| Barren/sparsely vegetated | 1.00 | 1.00 |

These are eligibility fractions, not renewable power densities. The shipping
allocation is a further, separate multiplier. For example, approximately
50% suitable shrubland becomes approximately **1% of the cell allocated to
shipping** after applying 2%. Those two percentages describe the same landscape
at different accounting stages.

Supplementary Figure 6 visually shows high pre-allocation suitability in the
deserts and much of Australia. Its high values cannot be interpreted as the
post-2% shipping fraction, which cannot exceed 0.02. A numerical reproduction
should therefore compare the pre-shipping suitability map to that figure,
not the final shipping land budget.

The MODIS product description matters: native MCD12Q1 is 500 m, whereas MCD12C1
contains class fractions on a 0.05-degree grid, roughly 5.6 km north–south.
Neither is the approximately 250 m product implied by the paper's prose.
[NASA product descriptions](https://modis-land.gsfc.nasa.gov/landcover.html).
The earlier Salmon and Bañares-Alcántara 2022 paper explicitly cites MCD12Q1
Collection 6 and identifies ETOPO1 as the slope source. This is a useful
provenance lead, not proof of the exact files used for the later shipping paper.

The available files are MCD12C1 **2022 Collection 6.1**, **WDPA February 2026**,
and **GEBCO 2025**. The present reconstruction is consequently labelled
**source-substituted**, not exact historical replication. The public
[network-code deposit](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/v4yz7778mh/1)
expects a prepared production-cost/capacity table; it does not resolve the
missing land-construction provenance.

For the paper-method PV reconstruction I use 9 km2/GW at the equator and the
latitude dependence of van de Ven equation 6, with tilt equal to absolute
latitude. The 9 km2/GW normalization is an explicit reconstruction assumption,
not recovered original implementation code. The formula is not extrapolated
past its polar validity limit. The main engineering reference also distinguishes
array packing from utilization of the entire site, so an additional site factor
must not be counted twice.
[van de Ven et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-82042-5).

## Confirmed implementation issues

1. **One-degree latitude misalignment in the current HDF reader.** MODIS
   classes for a row labelled latitude `lat` come from `[lat-1, lat]`, while
   cell areas, protected areas and slopes use `[lat, lat+1]`. Reading the
   actual HDF metadata and reproducing the shifted calculation matches the
   current onshore fractions to 2.3e-16 and solar areas to 9.1e-13 km2 across
   the diagnostic sample. This is confirmed, not inferred from a map.
2. **Exclusions are combined after coarse aggregation.** Multiplying a
   protected-area factor by a slope factor assumes within-cell independence.
   The correct operation is their spatial union/intersection with the land
   classes before aggregation. Overlapping exclusions must not be counted
   twice. Errors can increase or decrease land availability.
3. **Elevation sign is used as a land test in the current slope calculation.**
   Below-sea-level land is not necessarily water. The new pilot uses MODIS
   classes to exclude water and retains flat negative-elevation terrain.
4. **PV packing is not a literal implementation of the cited equation.**
   The current row-spacing expression exchanges the sine/cosine projections,
   uses module width relative to a length-based pitch, and adds tilt/spacing
   clamps. Moreover, the plant runner recalculates the density from the YAML,
   rather than simply consuming the value in the land CSV. At 23 degrees,
   the old CSV fixed-PV density is 78.05 MW/km2, the runtime value is
   73.60 MW/km2, and the reconstructed paper density is 83.45 MW/km2.
   The current tracking runtime density is 36.80 MW/km2 because of the
   YAML's 2x footprint multiplier.

The runtime now supports a tested, explicit `explicit_fixed_pv_density_v1`
input contract. New versioned inputs retain their stated density; unversioned
historical inputs keep their existing behaviour. This is an opt-in integration
change, not a silent change to earlier results.

Two suspected errors were **not** found: the current onshore Table 2 factors
are correct, and the current shipping fraction is applied once, not twice.
MCD12C1 labels water as layer 0; this is intentional and is not an off-by-one
mistake relative to the native product's class-17 water label.

An older local October 2023 notebook is a separate concern. It contains an
east–west-distance-squared area calculation, comments rather than implemented
slope/protection exclusions, water suitability, and regression-based ammonia
capacity filling. Its generated table contains 1,223 negative capacities.
That table must not be used as a physical land input. Its provenance is **not
established as the source of the original paper supplier table**, so these
notebook faults cannot responsibly be attributed to the published analysis.

## Completed numerical checks

### Joint-mask pilot

Both ARC jobs completed successfully: 8806853 (centered cells, 7m52s) and
8806854 (southwest-anchored cells, 14m03s). The latter spent about six minutes
waiting on a file read in its final cell; the compute-node process check
showed an I/O wait, and it subsequently completed normally.

The pilot uses 40 deliberately selected cells: the historical extremes,
Australian high-capacity locations, a geographic Australian sample, and the
three established benchmark sites. It is **not a representative global sample**.
All 80 cell results were fetched, content-hash verified and independently
rechecked for area conservation, bounds and overlapping exclusions.

The revised geometry applies the slope and protected masks together at native
GEBCO pixel centers, then aggregates with correct spherical area weights.
Because MCD12C1 does not reveal class positions within its 0.05-degree pixels,
the output includes a conditional-uniform estimate and sharp class-allocation
lower/upper bounds. This remains an approximation to a native class-mask
intersection, not a claim of exact 500 m land-cover reconstruction.

| Cell | Current solar land, km2 | Corrected centered, km2 | Corrected southwest, km2 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Atacama, -23/-69 | 220.30 | 226.84 | 217.71 |
| Northwest Australia, -23/117 | 108.39 | 113.13 | 109.18 |
| Central Australia, -21/135 | 109.69 | 102.44 | 109.60 |
| Lake Eyre diagnostic, -29/137 | 7.03 | 106.41 | 11.14 |

All these figures are **after** the 2% shipping allocation. Across the 36
sample cells with positive current solar area, the centered median ratio is
1.0044; the southwest median is 1.0000. Some coastal or protected cells change
much more, and one zero-current cell becomes positive. The Lake Eyre example
also shows why the coordinate-anchor convention must be specified: a half-cell
shift can change which lake, shoreline and protected polygons are included.
Its 15x centered change must not be attributed solely to elevation sign.

### Capacity conversion is a separate and larger discrepancy

Holding September's replication plant designs fixed gives:

| Cell | Archived capacity, Mt/year | September replication | Corrected land, 2x tracking footprint | Corrected land, fixed packing only |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Atacama | 9.769 | 2.316 | 2.704 | 5.408 |
| Northwest Australia | 4.020 | 0.955 | 1.130 | 2.261 |
| Central Australia | 4.324 | 0.459 | 0.437 | 0.489 |

The final column retains tracking generation and installed powers. It is not
the result of optimizing a fixed-PV plant. Even this favourable packing-only
counterfactual does not reproduce the historical capacities. Central Australia
is especially informative: its replication plant requires about 184.6 km2 of
wind spacing per 1 Mt/year, so reducing PV footprint does little to its total
land requirement. The historical 4.324 Mt/year would require about 905 km2
under that same design and fixed packing, versus about 102 km2 available.

I also reaggregated MODIS for **all 15,377 unique historical supplier
coordinates**, using both anchor conventions. In a deliberately favourable
ablation, I removed all protected/slope exclusions, retained the Table 2
factors and 2% share, and applied fixed-PV packing to September replication
designs:

- Of 12,774 matched cells with positive capacities in both datasets, the
  historical capacity exceeds this same-design reconstruction at **11,727
  (91.8%)**. The median reconstructed/historical capacity ratio is **0.440**.
- In Australia, this holds at **622 of 668 cells (93.1%)**, with a median
  ratio of **0.384**.
- Removing the 21 extreme heuristic flags barely changes the global median:
  **0.441** among the remaining matched cells.

Therefore the discrepancy is not confined to the extreme outliers, nor can
protected/slope exclusions alone explain it. This is a conditional comparison
using the recovered replication designs: it does **not** prove that the
original plants had the same renewable mix, or identify a unique error in the
unrecovered historical conversion code.

Separately, 21 archived cells exceed a deliberately generous sanity check:
2% of the entire cell, no class/protection/slope restrictions, equatorial
111.1 MW/km2 PV density everywhere, 100% annual generation and only 5 MWh/t
ammonia electricity demand. The largest archived value is 356.47 Mt/year;
the check gives about 44.94 Mt/year there. This is an optimistic engineering
heuristic, not a universal thermodynamic bound or a proposal for replacing
values by clipping. Original values remain preserved for archival replay.

## Recommended revised land model

1. **Separate physical suitability from shipping allocation.** Store raw
   class areas, protected/slope intersections, technology-suitable areas, and
   the post-allocation areas separately. Keep 2% for controlled replication
   and comparisons, but label it a scenario rather than an observed property
   of every cell. Do not use 50% or 100% simply to restore an old network.
2. **Use native class masks for the promoted version.** Reproduce with the
   best-recovered MODIS Collection 6 / historical WDPA / ETOPO1 inputs. Build
   the revised stack from a separately pinned modern snapshot. Use a suitable
   land DEM, explicit nodata/water treatment and aligned joint masks. The
   current GEBCO/CMG pilot is a useful test and interim sensitivity, not the
   final spatial-resolution standard.
3. **Keep eligibility assumptions honest.** Bare ground and shrubland are
   not automatically environmentally or socially available. Protected-area
   status, point-only records, settlements, setbacks, terrain and fragmented
   patches need explicit policies. The pilot uses designated/inscribed/
   established non-marine polygons and does not silently invent buffers for
   point-only WDPA records.
4. **Use fixed PV as a candidate central case, with consistent units and
   whole-site accounting.** Establish whether the generation/cost capacity
   basis is MWdc or MWac before importing empirical density numbers. Calibrate
   the proposed layout against actual projects; distinguish array spacing
   from roads, setbacks and ancillary plant area. The paper's 9 km2/GW is a
   replication assumption, not automatically the best modern whole-site value.
5. **Test tracking with its own output and cost.** Bolinger and Bolinger's
   empirical 2019 benchmarks are approximately 86.5 MWdc/km2 fixed and
   59.3 MWdc/km2 tracking for direct array area, a ratio around 1.46 rather
   than a universal 2. Their energy-density comparison is less decisive.
   These are calibration checks, not universal 2050 Australian defaults.
   [Empirical PV land study](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2022-01/lbnl_ieee-land-requirements-for-utility-scale-pv.pdf).
6. **Do not treat a minimum-LCOA plant's scaled capacity as the only feasible
   design.** Offer a small supply curve or land-constrained re-optimization.
   A slightly more expensive, solar-heavier plant can use less land than the
   unconstrained cost minimum. Distinguish gross wind-farm spacing from direct
   turbine disturbance and test explicit wind/PV co-location; neither full
   overlap nor fully exclusive use should be implicit.

At the Green Porpoise stage, hold demand, routes and selection rules fixed for
land-only comparisons. Report country production together with capacity and
supplier counts around the 1 Mt/year threshold. A modest capacity change can
switch many suppliers on or off and cause a large national-network response.
Australia's output should be diagnosed through those switches, not constrained
to stay near the historical result.

## Deliverables and remaining work

The two stacks are separately configured under `replication/` and `revised/`.
Each currently has a validated 40-cell input under the new campaign, with
full area diagnostics and provenance. They deliberately share corrected
geography and paper packing at this controlled-comparison stage; separate
directories do not imply a scientifically justified difference in land area.

The first full-year hourly fixed-PV diagnostic, **8807144**, failed before
optimization: the staged source omitted the parent of its technology YAML
overlay. Its extracted weather and logs are preserved, but it provides no
plant result. Staging now resolves the complete inherited configuration and
has an early preflight check. A separately versioned rerun uses the same
central cost/physics assumptions and corrected common land. **Rerun 8809820
has now completed and passed independent QA.** Fixed PV increases capacity
by 51.8% in Atacama and 55.1% in northwest Australia on the same corrected
land, at LCOA increases of 5.3% and 4.6%. Central Australian capacity changes
only +0.2%, because increased wind footprint offsets the reduced PV footprint.
Historical capacities remain substantially higher. See the
[completed fixed-PV report](FIXED_PV_20260914.md) for the matched comparison,
tracking-spacing sensitivity and limitations.

Next gates are: recover native/historical source identities; complete a
land-constrained design sensitivity; validate
representative coast/protected/terrain cases at native resolution; then build
global replication and revised land inputs, and only then rerun the controlled
Green Porpoise comparisons. The original land-to-capacity code or intermediate
per-cell class/renewable capacities would greatly strengthen attribution of
the remaining historical gap. No global reconstructed land stack has yet been
promoted, and no earlier result has been overwritten.
