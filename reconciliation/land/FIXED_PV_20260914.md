# Fixed PV versus tracking: completed three-site diagnostic

## Conclusion

Fixed PV increases land-limited supply substantially at the two solar-led
sites, but raises their optimized ammonia cost. It hardly changes supply at
the wind-heavy central Australian site. Correcting land/density and using
fixed PV therefore explains part, not all, of the historical capacity gap.
These are three diagnostic locations, not a global Australian result.

Keep fixed PV as a transparent revised-model reference. Before promoting a
global surface, test land-constrained plant choices or a small cost–quantity
curve: the cheapest unconstrained plant is not necessarily the best design
for a land-limited supplier.

## Results on common corrected land

Capacities are Mt NH3/year. LCOA is **EUR 2020 per tonne**, not the USD 2018
supplier-export basis. The main comparison retains the current assumption
that tracking needs twice the area per MW of fixed PV.

| Site | Central tracking-dominated capacity | Fixed-PV capacity | Capacity change | Central LCOA | Fixed LCOA |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Atacama | 2.699 | 4.096 | +51.8% | 222.83 | 234.71 (+5.3%) |
| Northwest Australia | 1.125 | 1.746 | +55.1% | 240.81 | 251.86 (+4.6%) |
| Central Australia | 0.3760 | 0.3768 | +0.2% | 208.64 | 213.17 (+2.2%) |

The hourly profiles give tracking approximately 27.3%, 24.5% and 25.5% more
annual electricity per MW, respectively. That gain does not offset a 100%
footprint premium at the solar-led sites. However, fixed PV also needs a
different optimized plant: simply halving the old tracking footprint would
overstate the capacity improvement.

At central Australia, switching to fixed reduces the reference plant's PV
footprint from **41.1 to 24.1 km2**, but the optimizer increases wind capacity
from **1,156.6 to 1,239.2 MW**. Wind footprint rises from **231.3 to 247.8 km2**.
Those effects almost cancel, so total footprint and scalable output barely
change. This is a plant-design/land interaction, not evidence that PV packing
has no effect elsewhere.

The central comparator already allowed fixed PV. Removing tracking therefore
narrows its feasible choices and should not lower its unconstrained minimum
cost. The benefit of fixed in this experiment is greater supply per unit of
land, not cheaper production per tonne.

## Sensitivity to tracker spacing

A footprint ratio of **1.5** instead of 2 reduces fixed PV's capacity advantage
to **13.8% in Atacama** and **16.5% in northwest Australia**. At central
Australia, fixed has **3.6% less** scalable capacity than the central design
under this alternative ratio. LCOA does not change in this packing-only
sensitivity because the designs are held fixed and land has no rental cost.

This sensitivity is not a calibrated global tracking-density replacement.
MWdc/MWac conventions, array versus whole-site area, setbacks and actual layout
must be resolved before promoting a revised density assumption.

## What this explains about the historical discrepancy

| Site | Archived historical capacity | Earlier central, original land/density | Central, corrected common land/density | Fixed, corrected common land/density |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Atacama | 9.769 | 2.312 | 2.699 | 4.096 |
| Northwest Australia | 4.020 | 0.951 | 1.125 | 1.746 |
| Central Australia | 4.324 | 0.397 | 0.376 | 0.377 |

The new fixed capacities remain only about **42%, 43% and 9%** of the
historical capacities, respectively. The remaining discrepancy is not
resolved by this switch. The global class-only audit also found a widespread
gap even after removing the extreme outliers; that remains a separate line
of evidence, not something to extrapolate from these three cells.

Northwest Australia crosses the 1 Mt/year supplier threshold already under
the corrected common land/density with the central design. Fixed PV then
increases its margin above the threshold. Central Australia remains below it
in every case here. A Green Porpoise national-output change must therefore be
explained through supplier eligibility, available quantity, cost and routing,
not through packing alone.

## Next reconciliation gates

1. **Green Lory: test constrained design choices.** At these same sites,
   construct a small supply curve with both PV types and wind available under
   the common land constraint. Hold costs, weather and allocation fixed. Test
   whether solar-heavier designs can offer more supply at an acceptable cost,
   especially at the central Australian cell; do not assume they can.
2. **Build the two native-resolution land stacks separately.** Recover
   historical MODIS/WDPA/DEM identities and intermediate class areas where
   possible; label any substitution explicitly. For the revised stack, use
   aligned native class/protection/slope masks, explicit pixel areas and
   nodata/water handling, and engineering-calibrated PV packing. The current
   40-cell CMG pilot is not an exact historical or final global input.
3. **Green Porpoise: make controlled network comparisons after global QA.**
   Keep MOD-AMB RCP4.5/70%, costs, demand, routes and supplier rules fixed.
   Separate archival replay, paper-method reconstruction and revised supply.
   Report country capacity, suppliers around the cutoff, delivered cost and
   flows. Do not tune land assumptions to force historical Australian output.
