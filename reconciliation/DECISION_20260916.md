# Legacy-lcoa versus green-lory: comparison and decision

16 September 2026. This document consolidates the reconciliation of the old
`lcoa-opt` plant model ("legacy-lcoa") with the current PyPSA implementation
("green-lory"), the resulting green-porpoise shipping networks, and the
decision on which model to use. Numbers are traceable to the campaign files
named in section 8; nothing here is re-derived by hand. Mt/yr means million
tonnes of ammonia per year; USD/t means USD2018 per tonne unless stated.

## 1. The question and the answer

The green-porpoise supplier distribution changed radically when its historical
supplier input (`c_NH3_cost_4.5.csv`, Salmon 2023) was replaced by a green-lory
surface: Australia fell from 258 Mt/yr of production to 25 Mt/yr (historical-
style green-lory) or 1 Mt/yr (hourly central green-lory), and delivered cost
rose from about 260 to 275 or 310 USD/t. The reconciliation had to establish
why, reproduce the legacy model end to end, and decide which model is more
usable and realistic. The legacy model is now reproduced end to end: its
replicated supplier table gives a network with Australia at 250 Mt/yr and the
archived producer ranking at 268 USD/t (network run E), against 255 to 259
Mt/yr and 260 to 261 USD/t with the archived table itself.

**Finding.** The divergence is not primarily a plant-cost difference. The
legacy model, replicated from its frozen 3 May 2023 source, reproduces the
archived costs to a uniform +4 %; green-lory in its historical-assumption mode
reproduces them to −1 % at the median. The divergence comes from the
**land-to-capacity step**: the archived `Max_capacity` column is reproduced by
a rule with an effective PV density of 140 MW/km2 (7.1 km2/GW) without latitude
dependence, 7.3 MW/km2 of wind, complete wind/solar overlap on the same 2 %
land share and no protected-area or slope exclusion. The September green-lory
land method (83 MW/km2 fixed PV at 23 degrees with latitude adjustment,
tracking at twice the fixed footprint, 5 MW/km2 wind, exclusive shared land,
slope and protected-area exclusions) gives capacities 0.22 times the archived
values at the median. Applying the recovered legacy rule to green-lory's own
replication costs restores Australia to 238.5 Mt/yr in the network (network run
D), against 255 to 259 Mt/yr with the archived table itself. The plant-cost
premium of the hourly central configuration (+12.6 % at the median, with
identified mechanisms) is a second, smaller and better-founded effect.

**Decision.** Use **green-lory** as the model of record: the hourly central
plant configuration for costs, with capacity taken from land-enforced
finite-site solves (cost against quantity) rather than from a scaled cheapest
design. Use the **legacy-lcoa package only as the replication baseline** for
the published network. Do not adopt the recovered legacy capacity constants
as a physical method, and do not treat the September scaled-design capacity
as a site maximum. Section 7 gives the reasoning and the caveats.

What the cells show:

- Costs agree across all three models within a few per cent in the replication
  modes. The central premium (+31 USD/t at the two PV-dominated cells, +11 at
  the wind-rich cell) is the sum of hourly resolution and ramp accounting
  (+9 to +11), the explicit compressor (+7 to +23), tank-only storage (−1 to −4)
  and water (+3), in that order.
- Capacity is where the models part. The same land (about 227 / 113 / 115 km2
  of 2 % suitable area) gives 9.4 / 4.0 / 2.4 Mt/yr under the legacy rule and
  2.3 / 1.0 / 0.4 Mt/yr under the September method, a factor 4 to 6.
- Under corrected land, the archived capacities are about twice the annual
  energy that the cell's 2 % land can generate: 4.5, 2.0 and 2.0 Mt/yr are the
  highest solved feasible quantities; 5.0, 2.5 and 4.3 Mt/yr are infeasible by
  solver or by energy certificate.
- Central Australia's strong wind (52 % net capacity factor) makes its cheapest
  design wind-heavy and therefore land-hungry; re-optimising the mix at a fixed
  1 Mt/yr keeps it eligible at 256 USD/t. The cheapest-design scaling that
  excluded it (0.4 Mt/yr) is a definition artefact, not a physical limit.
- In the networks, none of the three cells produces in the archived network or
  in runs A to A3 (all three are selected but idle); Atacama produces at its
  full capacity in runs B and C and 0.43 Mt/yr in runs D and E; the two
  Australian cells are absent from B and C and selected but idle in D and E.
  The cells are representative diagnostics; the network driver is the
  Australian pool as a whole.

## 5. Why the models differ: the causal chain

plant costs and operating constraints -> renewable/storage design ->
land-limited capacity -> supplier eligibility -> routing and storage ->
network cost and geography.

1. **Plant cost (small, explained).** green-lory replication mode matches the
   archived surface at −0.97 % (median over 14,035 cells); the legacy rerun is
   +3.8 %. The central mode is +12.6 % at the median over the replication-eligible
   cells, decomposed at the focal cells as above. These are documented modelling
   choices, not errors.
2. **Design (moderate, cell-dependent).** The frozen legacy plant and green-lory
   replication choose different wind shares at wind-rich cells (central
   Australia 348 versus 923 MW wind per Mt/yr) because component costs differ
   (H2 storage, battery interface, compressor). Under a wind-limited capacity
   rule this changes capacity by up to a factor 2.7 at such cells, which is why
   the legacy rule on green-lory designs gives 0.91 Mt/yr at central Australia
   while the same rule on legacy designs gives 2.42.
3. **Capacity (dominant).** Land-conversion constants and sharing rule:
   140 versus 37-83 MW/km2 of PV, 7.3 versus 5 MW/km2 of wind, complete overlap
   versus exclusive shared land, no exclusions versus slope and protected-area
   exclusions. Globally the September method gives a median 0.22 of the archived
   capacity; the eligible pool falls from 4,548 cells to 1,253 (replication) or
   832 (central); Australia from 553 eligible cells to 22 or 1.
4. **Supplier selection (sharp).** The 1 Mt/yr cutoff converts the capacity
   difference into a change of the candidate set: 166 (replication) or 188
   (central) of the archived network's 234 active suppliers fall below it,
   carrying 408 or 474 Mt/yr of historical production.
5. **Network (consequence).** With the same deposited equations, demand and
   regenerated routing, delivered cost and Australian production are:

## 6. Realism of each model

**Plant model.** green-lory's central configuration is the better-specified
plant: hourly variability and ramping, an explicit compressor with its own
capital cost, a tank store priced from a datasheet, water, snapshot-weighted
accounting, and an energy audit (9.2 MWh/t, 68 % HHV) that closes. Its higher
cost has named causes. The legacy plant is a valid 2023 model of the same
chain at four-hour resolution with bundled storage and compression; it is not
wrong, but it is coarser, and its finance convention could only be matched
within 4 %.

**Capacity model.** The legacy constants are not physically defensible as a
method: 7.1 km2/GW of PV is within about 40 % of the bare module area of
commercial modules (5 to 6 km2/GW at 18 to 20 % efficiency), leaving almost no
row spacing, and it is 2.4 times denser than the 2019 median direct-array
density of single-axis tracking plants in Bolinger and Bolinger's empirical
update (0.24 MWdc per acre, 59 MW/km2; fixed tilt 0.35 MWdc per acre,
87 MW/km2); and no protected-area or slope exclusion is applied. Counting the
full 2 % budget for both wind and PV is, by contrast, close to right: a wind
farm's footprint is mostly open spacing, and only its direct-impact area
(about 3 %, Denholm et al. 2009) excludes co-located PV. The revised green-lory
rule (`colocated`, decided on 16 September) adopts exactly that, so the
legacy/green-lory gap is now attributed to PV density and exclusions. At the focal cells the resulting capacities
exceed by a factor 2 what the same land can generate in a year under corrected
assumptions. The archived Australian dominance (43 % of world supply) rests on
these constants. The September green-lory method corrects the geometry but has
its own weaknesses: the tracking footprint of twice fixed PV and the exclusive
shared-land rule are assumptions, the classwise union is a lower bound, the 2 %
share is uniform, and above all it scales one cheapest design instead of
optimising the site, which understates feasible quantity by a factor 2 to 5 at
the focal cells. The land-enforced finite-site solves fix the last point and
give cost-against-quantity curves; they are the right capacity method, and they
restore all three focal cells above the 1 Mt/yr cutoff.

**Network model.** The deposited equations are the reproduction reference. The
network follows the capacity input almost mechanically (run D), so the realism
of any network is bounded by the realism of its capacity column. Neither the
archived network (capacities twice the energy ceiling) nor the central network
with scaled-design capacities (Australia at 1 Mt/yr from a definition artefact)
should be presented as the realistic 2050 picture.

## 7. Decision

1. **Model of record: green-lory, central configuration**, for plant costs and
   designs. Keep the replication configuration as a regression baseline that
   reproduces the legacy costs.
2. **Capacity: land-enforced finite-site optimisation** (cost against
   quantity, or validated quantity blocks) on the corrected land stack, with
   the land rules decided on 16 September: wind and PV share the suitable-land
   budget (`colocated`, wind exclusive fraction 0.03 from Denholm et al. 2009,
   a better co-location reference remains a TODO), fixed-tilt PV as the
   central technology with tracking-only as a named sensitivity, and a tracking
   footprint of 1.46 times fixed tilt from Bolinger and Bolinger (2022) instead
   of 2. The September scaled-design capacity is a conservative lower bound,
   not the site maximum; the legacy constants are a reproduction device only.
3. **Legacy-lcoa: replication baseline only.** The package reproduces the
   published supplier input within stated residuals and should be cited as
   such when the paper network is reproduced. It is not to be used for new
   estimates.
4. **Networks:** report the archived replay, the legacy replicated network (run
   E: the reproduction of the published network with recovered constants) and
   the green-lory network side by side, with gaps, until the long runs deliver
   accepted results; consume quantity-dependent supply so that central
   Australia is not excluded by a scaling artefact; treat the 1 Mt/yr cutoff as
   a historical convention and test lower cutoffs as explicit model changes.

Conditions attached to the decision: both green-lory surfaces still assume Way
learning, reduced WACC, flat build and remoteness costs, no land rent, uniform
water cost and one weather year (2019); the alternative ERA5-derived profiles
give 7 to 9 % lower fixed-PV and 26 to 38 % lower raw wind capacity factors at
the focal cells and have not been propagated; PV profile normalisation above
1.0 is unresolved; the tracking footprint needs empirical validation. None of
these change the ranking between the models; all of them change absolute costs.

## Post-scriptum, 23 September 2026: land-table labelling bug

The green-lory land tables behind every surface cited above (July build
8253741, `max_capacities_paper_2pct_slope15.csv`) label each MODIS band by its
north edge while their exclusions and areas assume a south-west corner label.
The suitability of label (lat, lon) is therefore that of the cell one degree
south, combined with the exclusions of the labelled cell (verified exactly on
the September table; see RUNS.md, 23 September). This does not change the
decision (the capacity method, not the plant cost, drives the divergence, and
the legacy replication does not use these tables), but the September
green-lory capacities, the stated-method legacy variant's land input and the
availability ratio map (median 0.85) are affected and will be regenerated from
the centred build (`20260923-land-center-v1`). The corrected centred
aggregation reproduces the legacy land step to machine precision, so the
remaining green-lory/legacy land difference at 2 % is exactly the WDPA and
slope exclusion factor (interim estimate: median 0.89 per cell, 0.84
area-weighted; Australia 0.76, Sahara countries 0.94-0.99).
